# Fairlend Marketing

> Marketing-specific impeccable context for the `/marketing` route and public Fairlend pages. This file is intentionally separate from root `PRODUCT.md`, which remains the source of truth for authenticated DrawFlow product UI.

## Register

brand

## Users

Fairlend marketing pages speak to four overlapping public audiences:

- **Builder and developer borrowers:** small to mid-scale housing builders looking for construction draw financing, private mortgage options, and a lender that understands permits, milestones, evidence, site realities, and working-capital pressure.
- **Investors and capital partners:** investors evaluating real-estate-backed opportunities that must show underwriting discipline, impact proof, and operational maturity without consumer-fintech gloss.
- **Civic and community stakeholders:** housing advocates, municipal-adjacent readers, and partners who need to believe Fairlend creates durable homes, not just financial products.
- **Press, referral partners, and high-intent researchers:** people scanning for legitimacy, proof, leadership, program fit, and a coherent point of view.

Visitors are usually comparing trust signals quickly: who Fairlend is, what kind of housing it backs, how capital is governed, and whether this team understands both community outcomes and financial risk.

## Product Purpose

The marketing surface exists to position Fairlend as a housing-capital platform that finances and supports real Canadian housing with disciplined underwriting, transparent execution, and measurable community impact.

It should make three things unmistakable:

1. Fairlend funds physical homes, not abstract financial products.
2. Fairlend aligns investment discipline with community outcomes.
3. DrawFlow-style operational transparency is part of the brand promise, even when the visitor is not inside the application.

Success is a visitor taking the next serious step: explore build financing, evaluate investment opportunities, contact the team, read a guide, or trust Fairlend enough to continue deeper into the public site.

## Brand Personality

**Bold. Civic. Measured.**

Fairlend marketing should feel like civic housing infrastructure made legible. It has the authority of an underwriting memo, the tactile warmth of an architecture studio, and the public confidence of a housing institution that can explain what it builds.

Voice:

- **Direct:** concrete nouns, short declarations, no vague platform language.
- **Human:** people, homes, neighbourhoods, and builders stay visible beside capital and return language.
- **Disciplined:** financial claims are sober, specific, and paired with process.
- **Material:** copy and visuals should remind visitors this is about wood, concrete, permits, streets, families, and homes.

Primary brand line:

> Bold structure. Human housing. Lasting impact.

## Anti-References

Fairlend marketing must not look or sound like:

- **Generic fintech:** navy and gold trust theater, abstract charts, glossy gradients, smiling stock-office people, vague "capital solutions."
- **Generic real estate:** luxury condo brochure styling, lifestyle-only imagery, skyline hero shots, passive investment hype.
- **Generic SaaS:** centered hero stack, hero-metric template, identical icon-card grids, abstract feature copy, decorative product mockups.
- **Generic construction:** hard-hat clichés, yellow caution-tape palette, muddy job-site photography without investment or civic context.
- **Consumer-app friendliness:** bubbly illustrations, excessive rounded pills, confetti moments, cute onboarding language.
- **Overly quiet beige editorial:** the brand uses paper and warmth, but it needs structure, contrast, and point of view.

Absolute pattern bans still apply: gradient text, decorative glassmorphism, colored side-stripe card accents, nested cards, modal-as-first-thought, and all-caps body copy.

## Design Principles

1. **Lead with the real thing.** First viewports need actual housing, people, plans, or investment artifacts, not abstract atmosphere.
2. **Make capital visible but accountable.** Metrics belong beside what they produced: homes financed, affordability, emissions avoided, draw transparency, review discipline.
3. **Use the grid as evidence.** Visible rules, section numbers, blueprint overlays, and stamps should make the page feel measured and inspected.
4. **Balance institutional and human warmth.** Black Green, Civic Blue, and blueprint systems create authority; Paper White, material texture, and community photography keep it grounded.
5. **Every CTA is a serious next step.** Buttons should feel like forward motion in a financing process, not marketing fluff.
6. **Marketing can be louder than the app.** Public pages may use compressed type, full palette sections, image-led compositions, and scroll choreography. Authenticated DrawFlow UI stays operational.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA minimum across public pages.
- Do not rely on color alone for investment status, availability, warning, eligibility, or program type.
- Preserve clear keyboard focus states on navigation, CTAs, cards, forms, and menus.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, especially on GSAP scroll scenes and hero reveals.
- Keep mobile tap targets at least 44px.
- Avoid long all-caps copy. Use uppercase only for short labels, stamps, section numbers, and compressed display phrases.
- Write in plain, domain-specific language. Builder and investor audiences should not need jargon decoding.
