FairLend Landing Page Hero Synthesis

Business context:
FairLend is a Canadian brokerage and investment company serving builder/developer borrowers, private mortgage clients, and investors. The hero needs to preserve the corporate/brokerage trust of the broader FairLend page while importing the higher-converting project-review flow from the build-financing page.

Source page strengths to integrate:
- Page 1 communicates company category, licensed brokerage posture, Canadian real-estate credibility, transparent terms, and technology-led underwriting.
- Page 1 uses a premium editorial split: large FairLend identity, deep green serif headline, beige background, realistic Canadian multiplex/urban housing imagery, blueprint overlay, and a license/business proof card.
- Page 2 converts builders/developers with a focused build-financing promise: "Build financing that moves with the work", progress-based reimbursement draws, no-credit-check/no-documents-required/2-minute chips, Step 1 of 7 progress stepper, blueprint-to-completed-building metaphor, and primary "Start project review" CTA with secondary "I'm just exploring".

Audience:
Canadian builders, developers, borrower/operators, and real-estate investors who need construction draw financing but also need to trust FairLend as a licensed brokerage and investment company.

Brand and layout constraints:
- Preserve FairLend's deep forest green, warm off-white/beige, restrained coral conversion accent, editorial serif headline feel, squared/low-radius premium UI, and blueprint/construction visual language.
- Use the FAiRLEND/FairLend brokerage identity as a brand cue, but do not rely on exact generated text fidelity.
- Include one clear primary conversion path for builders/developers and one quieter investor/company proof cue.
- Integrate license/proof/underwriting signals without burying the project-review CTA.
- Each output must be one unique desktop landing page hero mockup, not a collage, mood board, contact sheet, grid of pages, or multi-screen presentation.
- Keep UI text sparse and large enough to appear plausible. Avoid dense paragraphs, fake awards, discount language, mortgage-rate claims, unverified statistics, competitor logos, or unreadable microcopy.
